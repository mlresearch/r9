abstract: Approximate Bayesian inference is NP-hard. Dagum and Luby defined the Local
  Variance Bound (LVB) to measure the approximation hardness of Bayesian inference
  on Bayesian networks, assuming the networks model strictly positive joint probability
  distributions, i.e. zero probabilities are not permitted. This paper introduces
  the k-test to measure the approximation hardness of inference on Bayesian networks
  with deterministic causalities in the probability distribution, i.e. when zero conditional
  probabilities are permitted. Approximation by stochastic sampling is a widely-used
  inference method that is known to suffer from inefficiencies due to sample rejection.
  The k-test predicts when rejection rates of stochastic sampling a Bayesian network
  will be low, modest, high, or when sampling is intractable.
