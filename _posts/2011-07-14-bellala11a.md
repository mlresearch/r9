abstract: The problem of active diagnosis arises in several applications such as disease
  diagnosis, and fault diagnosis in computer networks, where the goal is to rapidly
  identify the binary states of a set of objects (e.g., faulty or working) by sequentially
  selecting, and observing, (noisy) responses to binary valued queries. Current algorithms
  in this area rely on loopy belief propagation for active query selection. These
  algorithms have an exponential time complexity, making them slow and even intractable
  in large networks. We propose a rank-based greedy algorithm that sequentially chooses
  queries such that the area under the ROC curve of the rank-based output is maximized.
  The AUC criterion allows us to make a simplifying assumption that significantly
  reduces the complexity of active query selection (from exponential to near quadratic),
  with little or no compromise on the performance quality.
