abstract: A database of objects discovered in houses in the Roman city of Pompeii
  provides a unique view of ordinary life in an ancient city. Experts have used this
  collection to study the structure of Roman households, exploring the distribution
  and variability of tasks in architectural spaces, but such approaches are necessarily
  affected by modern cultural assumptions. In this study we present a data-driven
  approach to household archeology, treating it as an unsupervised labeling problem.
  This approach scales to large data sets and provides a more objective complement
  to human interpretation.
