abstract: Prediction markets provide an efficient means to assess uncertain quantities
  from forecasters. Traditional and competitive strictly proper scoring rules have
  been shown to incentivize players to provide truthful probabilistic forecasts. However,
  we show that when those players can cooperate, these mechanisms can instead discourage
  them from reporting what they really believe. When players with different beliefs
  are able to cooperate and form a coalition, these mechanisms admit arbitrage and
  there is a report that will always pay coalition members more than their truthful
  forecasts. If the coalition were created by an intermediary, such as a web portal,
  the intermediary would be guaranteed a profit.
